# Decisions log

What we tried, what we chose, and why. This feeds the notebook's "evidence of development" section and the viva.

| # | Date | Decision | Why | Alternatives considered |
|---|------|----------|-----|-------------------------|
| 1 | 23 Oct | **FastAPI + HTML/JS** for the app, not Streamlit | Vercel runs FastAPI as a serverless function. Streamlit needs a permanent WebSocket connection, which Vercel functions do not support. | Streamlit (not deployable on Vercel); Streamlit Cloud (the brief requires Vercel) |
| 2 | 23 Oct | **No machine-learning libraries in the deployed app.** Product embeddings are computed once in Colab and saved as a file; at search time the app makes one embedding API call per query. | Vercel limits function size; PyTorch and local models are too large and slow to start. | Local embedding model on Vercel (size and cold-start risk) |
| 3 | 23 Oct | **Fixed pipeline, not an agent with tool-calling.** Steps: understand, retrieve, filter, re-rank, decide, answer. | Every search follows the same steps and takes no action in any system (Phase 1, row 4a). A fixed pipeline is cheaper, faster and easier to evaluate step by step. | LLM agent choosing tools (adds cost and unpredictability, no benefit here) |
| 4 | 23 Oct | **Session behaviour is held in the shopper's browser only.** The model receives only the query and a coarse summary (category, product types and price band viewed), never an ID. | Matches the Phase 1 commitment (row 6b). Avoids profiling as defined in the draft PDPB 2023, s. 2(dd). | Server-side session store (unneeded; adds privacy exposure) |
| 5 | 23 Oct | **Catalogue is fully synthetic, written by Claude (Anthropic)** as hand-authored data plus a deterministic script (`data/build_catalogue.py`, seed 722). 195 products, 4 categories, 144 product types, 49 types with 2-3 price variants, 11% out of stock. Fictional store "Sahulat Home" and fictional brands. | The brief forbids real confidential data. A script makes the data reproducible. Price variants allow budget and personalisation tests; stock-outs allow constraint tests. | Public e-commerce dataset (licensing and download issues, and not Pakistani household goods) |
| 6 | 23 Oct | **Catalogue text is feature-led; the utility wording lives only in the AI-generated definitions.** | This mirrors real listings and makes the experiment meaningful: if the catalogue already described uses, keyword search would look artificially good and the definitions would show no gain. **Caveat to state honestly:** we wrote the catalogue to avoid use-language, which may make the keyword baseline somewhat weaker than on a real store. | Utility-led descriptions in the catalogue (would undermine the comparison) |
| 7 | 24 Oct | **AI providers (confirmed by student, Option 1):** Groq for the app's chat model; Gemini free tier for embeddings and as the judge; OpenAI ($5) as a backup only. | Target is $0 running cost. Groq offers no embeddings, so Gemini covers them. A judge from a different company from the answering model gives a more independent grade. **Risk:** free-tier quotas are unpublished or reported only; to be checked on the student's own limits pages. | OpenAI only ($5 minimum prepaid); Gemini only (very low reported daily limit on the stronger model) |
| 8 | 23 Oct | **Evaluation queries are written by Claude (Anthropic)**, a different model family from the app's model. | The brief requires the query writer to differ from the answering model. | Same model writing and answering (self-grading bias) |
| 9 | 24 Oct | **Keyword baseline = BM25, written in plain Python** (`search/keyword.py`) over title, brand, category, subcategory and description. | BM25 is the standard keyword-ranking method behind most store search engines, so it is a fair "today" baseline. No extra libraries keeps the app small and easy to explain. First manual check: "egg whites from yolks" finds the egg separators (the words overlap); "pyaz barik katne wali cheez" (Roman Urdu) and "slice potatoes thin for chips" return nothing, which is the problem the project addresses. | A library (rank-bm25) gives the same maths with more dependencies |
| 10 | 24 Oct | **One shared search package** (`search/`) used by both the live app and the notebook. | The evaluation scores then describe the deployed system, not a copy of it. | Separate notebook code (risk of scoring something different from what is live) |
| 11 | 24 Oct | **Website: a single page with hash routing, plus a visible "This visit" panel** showing exactly what the search knows about the session and what is sent with a search. Session memory uses `sessionStorage` (this tab only). | The panel makes the personalisation transparent to shoppers (and to the marker in the demo), and supports the privacy position in decision 4. | Hidden tracking (less honest, harder to demo) |
| 12 | 24 Oct | **Groq model check:** a one-word reply used 104 tokens, because gpt-oss "reasons" silently before answering and those tokens count toward the quota. Plan: use a low reasoning-effort setting. | Free-tier token budget. | - |
| 13 | 24 Oct | **Groq limits verified** on the student's account (organization limits page): gpt-oss-120b = 30 requests/min, 1,000/day, 8,000 tokens/min, 200,000 tokens/day. The shared code paces itself to stay under 85% of the per-minute token cap and waits on rate-limit errors instead of failing. | The per-minute token cap is the binding constraint (about 5-6 searches/minute). | Upgrade to a paid tier (not needed for this volume) |
| 14 | 24 Oct | **Definition prompts: v1 (simple) then v2 (grounded and structured).** v2 rules: facts only, no safety/health/durability/marketing claims, structured fields (what it is, uses, shopper phrases, Urdu/Roman Urdu names). An automatic check flags claim words that are not in the product's facts. Products are sent 5 per call to cut prompt overhead. | Unsupported claims are a deceptive-marketing risk (CCP guidelines, Section 10 of the Competition Act 2010); structured fields can be checked part by part; local names support Urdu and Roman Urdu queries. | One product per call (more tokens); free-text definitions (harder to check) |
| 15 | 24 Oct | **Embeddings: gemini-embedding-001 at 768 dimensions**, with separate task types for products (RETRIEVAL_DOCUMENT) and queries (RETRIEVAL_QUERY). Two indexes: catalogue text, and title + definition. | 001 is generally available and was verified in Colab. 768 is a Google-recommended size and keeps each index about 1.2 MB, so the website can search it in plain Python. The two indexes let the evaluation isolate what the definitions add. | gemini-embedding-2 (newer, untested here; possible later comparison); 3072 dimensions (4x the file size) |
| 16 | 24 Oct | **One notebook per part** (01 definitions and embeddings, 02 evaluation, ...), each run in Colab and saved back to GitHub with outputs. Colab pushes results to the repo with a fine-grained GitHub token limited to this one repository. Every notebook was test-run end to end against simulated AI services before hand-over. | Smaller notebooks are less likely to lose work when Colab disconnects. The token removes manual file uploads. Simulated test runs meet the "works first time" requirement. | One big notebook; downloading and uploading files by hand |
| 17 | 24 Oct | **Reframed as a regular marketplace with an AI search feature** (student's direction). The home page is now a normal storefront; utility search is a labelled "AI search" section, and the header search box handles both product names and described needs. | Sahulat is a normal store; generative search is a feature it offers, not its identity. | Utility-only framing (v0) |
| 18 | 24 Oct | **Hybrid retrieval for the search box:** keyword (BM25) and meaning-based (definition embeddings) results merged with Reciprocal Rank Fusion. Falls back to keyword search, and tells the shopper, if the AI side is unavailable. | Meaning-based search alone can mis-rank exact product names; keyword alone misses described needs. RRF keeps both strengths and is simple to explain. Fallback keeps the marker's link working if a free quota runs out. New evaluation check: name searches must not get worse. | Semantic-only (risk on name searches); weighted score sum (needs score calibration) |
| 19 | 24 Oct | **Two entry points, one engine:** the search bar (no chat-model call: fast, free) plus an "Ask Sahulat" chat panel (Sunday) for vague or Urdu needs, follow-ups, constraints and explanations. The chat knows the page it was opened from. | Most shoppers use a search bar; the chat model's quota (8,000 tokens/min) is spent only where it adds value; matches the instructor's "opened from" wording. | Bot only (slower and costlier for simple searches); search only (no multi-turn refinement) |
| 20 | 24 Oct | **Images: line illustrations drawn in code** (`static/illustrations.js`): one per subcategory, plus specific drawings for 22 product types likely to appear in the demo. A **"What it's for" box** on product pages shows the AI uses, labelled as AI-summarised; it is switched off until the hand-check is done (`SHOW_WHAT_ITS_FOR` in app.py). | $0 and no copyright or brand risk. AI-generated photos could depict features a product lacks, which is the same unsupported-claim risk as in text. | AI-generated photos (paid, claim risk); stock photos (hours of manual matching) |
| 21 | 24 Oct | **Bug found and fixed: budget words polluted matching.** "emergency light under 2000" matched "Under-Bed Storage Bag" on the word "under". Fix: the rule-based constraint reader now removes the budget and stock phrase, and the remaining text is used for both keyword and meaning-based matching (`parse_constraints` returns `clean_query`). | Found while testing the hybrid pipeline. Candidate for the notebook's "failure found and fixed" section. | Leave as is (budget queries would rank wrong items) |
